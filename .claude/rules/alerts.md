# Alerts

## Triggers

- Saved per profile in `Profile.alerts` (`simultaneous`, `queue_length`, `items`).
- `AlertTriggerSchema` (`src/common/schemas/profile.ts`) derives the triggers from `TOKIO_EVENTS` (for a new event, see IPC events in `AGENTS.md`). `ROOM_USER` is excluded (it comes every few seconds and is meant for overlays), both there and in the `AlertTrigger` type. Saved Room Users alerts are dropped when a profile loads.
- `registerConnectorEvents` (`src/main/events/init.ts`) passes every trigger to `AlertsService.handle` while `activeFeatures.alerts` is on. Music Change alerts come from `MusicService.onSongChange`, once each time a song starts, not for one that fails to start, is skipped while it loads, or was already playing when a request came; the rest from the connector. `MUSIC_CHANGE` also fires when the queue changes, and overlays get every one through `ServerService.initEvents`. Gift alerts fire once per combo, on `repeatEnd`, or with `trigger.every_gift` (**Every gift**) once per gift sent, at most `queue_length` times per event. `GiftStreaks` (`src/main/helper/streaks.ts`, also used by `LiveService`) counts what each gift event adds to its sender's streak (handle and gift) like the gift jar overlay's `Tally`: the count's growth, the same count again before the end as the gift sent again, and nothing for a lower count within `LATE_MS` (it came late), with the streak's total so far. `AlertsService` runs it once per event, also with no alert on every gift, but only while alerts are on: one turned on mid-streak plays the whole count so far. `clear` forgets the streaks, since their ends may be missed.
- Matching is `matchesTrigger` (`src/main/helper/triggers.ts`, with `giftOf`), which `GamesService` shares for game actions (see `.claude/rules/games.md`): the type, the filters below, and gifts' `every_gift` against `added`. Both callers pass `added` and `total` from their `GiftStreaks`. `AlertsService` keeps `enabled` and the sound, message or media check. The trigger object is `TriggerSchema`; the Alerts page and Games both use `TriggerFilter` and `TRIGGER_OPTIONS` (`components/AlertItem`), Games without five of them (`GAME_TRIGGERS`). `TriggerFilter` renders nothing for a trigger without a filter.
- Two conditions in `TriggerSchema` that only the Alerts editor offers (Games' actions keep their defaults, which match everything):
  - `min_coins` (gifts, default 0): the coins the streak is worth when it ends (`gift.coins × total`), or each gift's `coins` with `every_gift`. Only for any gift: picked gifts are the alert's choice, so `matchesTrigger` ignores it then, and the editor and the summary leave it out.
  - `roles` (`VIEWER_ROLES`: `follower`, `moderator`, `superfan`, the `UserData` flags; default `[]`, anyone): the event's viewer has one of them, the chat's for Emote. `takesRoles` (`src/common/util/alerts.ts`, shared by the matcher, the editor and the summary) leaves out Follow (its viewer only just followed) and the events without a viewer (Envelope, Super Fan Box, Music Change): their roles are ignored and not offered.
- The Emote trigger (`EMOTE_TRIGGER`) isn't a live event: the loop skips it and `matchesTrigger` matches it on `CHAT` (empty `trigger.emotes` means any emote). TikTok can't list a LIVE's emotes, so `StoreManagerService.recordEmotes` saves the ones seen in chat to `Profile.emotes`, and main pushes the list with `EMOTES`. The Alerts page numbers them (`emoteOptions`) and picks them with `EmoteMultiSelect` (`components/EmoteSelect`): the gift picker's field and checked list, styled by `giftselect.module.css`, without the search, coins and sorting.
- Chat alerts filter on `trigger.keyword` through `hasKeyword` (`src/main/helper/chat.ts`, whole words through `wholeWord`, except next to letters of scripts written without spaces, such as Chinese, Japanese or Thai, where it matches anywhere; a keyword of only emojis is looked for as written), and Member Join and Super Fan Join alerts on `trigger.user` through `isUser` (handle or name).

## Gifts

- TikTok's gift list (`extra/gifts.json`) misses some gifts, like other regions' ones. `OverlayService.recordGift` saves the ones viewers send that aren't on it to `extra/custom-gifts.json`, shared by all profiles. It saves nothing while TikTok's list is empty, since every gift would look missing, and keeps both lists in memory once read, since every gift event checks them.
- The Alerts page reads them with `GET_CUSTOM_GIFTS`, and main pushes new ones on its reply, `CUSTOM_GIFTS`. `giftOptions` merges both lists cheapest first, each id once. Overlay gift settings use TikTok's list only.

## Page

- Each row (`AlertItem`) is the alert's switch, a summary button that opens its editor, Test (`alertTest(alert)`, shared with the editor) and Remove. The summary is the trigger's label and `triggerSummary` (one sentence: the gifts with `giftNames`, shared with `gameEventText`, when it plays, the coins, the roles), the sound with how long it plays or the message, and the media with its duration. The sound's length is `SoundLength`'s: `end − start` for a cut with an end, else the file's length (its hidden `<audio>`) less `from`, the cut's `start`. There are no columns: a new condition goes in the editor and the sentence.
- `AlertEditor` (`pages/Alerts`) is a `Modal` titled "Gift alert" (the trigger's label) with **Trigger**, **Sound** and **Media** tabs and **Done** in its footer. Its Test (`PlayButton`) is on the Sound tab: before **Reset cut** for a sound, before **Change...** for a message, none without either. Under it, the alert's `volume` ("Sound volume"), the sound's or message's only; a video has its own on the Media tab. Edits go straight to the page's `update`, saved like any change. The panel's `min-height` is the gift Trigger tab's, the tallest, so the dialog doesn't move between tabs. The pickers render after it, so they open over it, and Escape closes the picker first.

## Sounds

- Uploaded, browsed and downloaded sounds go to a library shared by all profiles, in `settings.sounds`. Files in the `sounds` folder are named by a hash of their content (uploads) or URL (MyInstants), and alerts store only the basename.
- Imports (`importSound`, `importMedia`) go through `importFile` (`src/main/helper/library.ts`): it copies the file under a hash of its content, and `GamesService` uses it for the Minecraft pictures too (see `.claude/rules/games.md`).
- Read them through `AlertsService.readSound` (keeps paths inside the folder, caches).
- Deleting a sound or media file (`REMOVE_SOUND`, `REMOVE_MEDIA`) takes it out of its library, then off every alert that has it in every profile (`StoreManagerService.removeFromAlerts`, which shares `_updateProfiles` with `removeFromGames`; the active profile through `updateProfileData`, which keeps it in memory), then deletes the file. A profile that can't be saved keeps the file, for its alerts, and fails with `saveFailure()`. Main sends `ALERT_FILE_REMOVED`, and the Alerts page clears the file from the alerts it shows, so its next save doesn't bring it back. Every delete button asks first (`Modal`, "Delete sound?" or "Delete media?"). **Clear** in the pickers takes the alert's sound, message or media off it and keeps the file.
- Upload counts only sounds new to the library: one already in `settings.sounds`, or picked twice, isn't counted. A removed sound whose file can't be deleted still leaves the library, with a warning (`SOUND_REMOVE_FAILED`).
- Sounds are up to `CACHE_MAX_FILE_BYTES` (8 MB): `importSound` and `fetchSound` refuse bigger ones, so every sound added now is served from memory. A bigger one saved before is read from disk, with one read shared by the events that want it at once. `handle` doesn't log a missing sound; `init.ts` logs it to `alerts.log` only when its notice goes out (once per 30s per alert).
- MyInstants has no API; `AlertsService` scrapes it with cheerio. Only fetch `www.myinstants.com` URLs.
- The sound lists (My sounds, MyInstants, Settings → Sound Library) show each sound's length with `SoundLength` (`components/SoundPicker`, `formatAdded`'s "2.5s" or "1:05"). A library file's length comes from a hidden `<audio preload="metadata">` loading `tokio-media:///sounds/<file>` (`getSoundPreviewURL`): `mediaFile` serves only audio right in the sounds folder there. A MyInstants sound not downloaded has a length once previewed: `useAlertTest` keeps each test's `lengths` from `onScheduled` (media tests replace it), and the Alerts page passes them to the picker. Nothing shows for a file that doesn't say its length.
- Main sends sounds with `ALERT_AUDIO` on a queue (`channel`), each with a `key` (its file, or its `tts:` cache key), `repeat`, the times it plays, and a file sound's cut (`start`, `end`): the window queues that many copies, sharing one decode, and forgets a `tts:` key's decoded audio (`forgetDecoded`) once its copies have places, since messages rarely repeat. Queues: `alerts`, or `alerts:<trigger>` per trigger when `simultaneous` is on. `ALERT_CLEAR` (on disconnect and when the feature turns off) stops every `alerts*` queue. Tests and previews use `useAlertTest` on the `alert-test` queue, so clearing never cuts them.
- The Alerts page applies a `SET_ALERT_SOUND` reply only while it waits for one for that alert (`onRequest`, from a Browse or MyInstants download), even after the picker closed; a library or TTS pick made since wins over a late reply. `onRequest` numbers each request, `DOWNLOAD_SOUND`/`IMPORT_SOUND` carry it as `request` and `SET_ALERT_SOUND` echoes it: a reply applies only if it's newer than the last one applied for that alert, and the picker shows only the newest request's failure. MyInstants loads its trending sounds only when its tab is first shown.

## Cuts

- An alert's sound is `AlertClipSchema`: the library's `{ name, file }` plus `start` (seconds, default 0) and `end` (null plays to the end). The library and the games overlay's pictures stay `AlertSound`. A pick from the library or a `SET_ALERT_SOUND` reply goes through `soundClip`: another file plays whole, the alert's own file keeps its cut. The file is never changed, so each alert cuts it its own way. Messages aren't cut.
- The window plays the part: `AudioQueue.enqueue`'s `start`/`end` (seconds of the sound) go to `GrainPlayer.start(time, start, length / speed)`, and the place lasts the part. A part outside the sound, or empty, plays it whole. A cut end fades over `CUT_FADE` (50 ms) on its own `Gain` node, so `setItemGain`'s ramps can't cancel it; a cut start needs nothing, as the player's grains fade in. Tests pass the cut through `AlertTestRequest`'s `start`/`end`, which main ignores.
- An alert's video is cut the same way: `AlertMediaSchema` shares the `start`/`end` fields (`CUT`), images ignore them, and `setMedia` keeps the cut for the same file and drops it for another. `SHOW_ALERT_MEDIA` carries them, `showMedia` refuses non-finite ones and clamps them, and the overlay page loads the video at `#t=<start>`. From its first `playing`, it times the video by its own clock: it hides it at `min(end, start + duration)` of `currentTime`, waking `stop − currentTime` seconds later and waiting again while the clock lags (a fragment's end is checked only every 250 ms). The page's `duration` timer hides only images and videos that aren't playing, since a video loads and starts after its turn, which ended the cut early.
- The Sound tab's waveform (`SoundCut`) asks main for the file's bytes with `GET_SOUND_DATA { file }` (`readSound`; `SOUND_DATA` echoes the file, failures included, `notify: false`: the tab says "Couldn't read this sound."), decodes them with `useAudio().decode` under the file's name, the key `ALERT_AUDIO` uses, so the editor and the alert's plays share one decode, and draws `waveform(buffer, 96)` (peaks over every channel, the loudest 1). **Reset cut** plays the whole sound again; it shows, with the Test, while the waveform loads or can't. Its handles are `role="slider"` ("Start", "End"): arrows move them by 0.1 s, Shift by 1 s, Home/End to the limits, and a press on the waveform moves the nearer one and drags it. They keep 0.1 s between them, and an end at the sound's end saves null. A Paper line sweeps the part while the alert's test plays (none under `prefers-reduced-motion`). The track and its fields are `CutTrack` (`SoundCut.tsx`) and the button `ResetCut`, which the Media tab shares.

## Text-to-speech

- An alert plays `sound` or speaks `tts` (a message with its own voice, speed and pitch). `tts` wins if both are set, and the picker clears one when the other is picked.
- `AlertsService.speak` fills the message with `formatAlertMessage` (variables per trigger in `ALERT_MESSAGE_VARIABLES`, `src/common/util/alerts.ts`), runs it through `TTSService.prepareText` (replacements, Remove Emojis, and banned words, which its third argument limits to the event's values, the template's variables filled, as chat TTS does) and generates it with the speaker from `setSpeaker`, caching it in the sound LRU under a `tts:` key.
- Alert queues are shared, so speed and pitch travel per sound on `ALERT_AUDIO.filters`, and `AudioQueue` items can override the queue's filters.
- `{count}` in a gift alert's message is the streak's total from `GiftStreaks`, not TikTok's `comboCount`. `TtsTab` doesn't offer it to an alert on every gift; a message that has it still gets the count so far. One event that adds several gifts makes the message once and sends one `ALERT_AUDIO` with `repeat`.
- Like chat TTS, alerts from live events make their messages through a `TaskQueue` with the TTS page's cooldown and queue length, so bursts can't flood the engine: blocked messages never enter it, and the ones past the length are dropped.
- `AlertsService.clear` runs wherever `ALERT_CLEAR` is sent: it drops the messages waiting, the sounds being read and the media waiting to show, hides the media on the overlays that showed some, and forgets the gift streaks. Previews call `speak` directly.

## Media

- An alert can also show an image or video (`media`: a file and a `duration`, 1–60 s), with its sound or message or alone. The library is shared by all profiles like the sounds: `settings.media`, files in the `media` folder named by a hash of their content, png, jpg, jpeg, gif, webp, mp4 or webm up to `MEDIA_MAX_BYTES` (50 MB). No SVG: opened from the overlay server, its scripts would run there. Media is never cached or sent over IPC. A rebuilt store recovers the library like the sounds' (`_findFiles`). The extensions and `mediaKind` are in `src/common/util/alerts.ts`, for the window too.
- Each alert has its own overlay at `getAlertOverlayURL(id)` (`/alerts/<alert id>/`): `ServerService` serves one page from memory (`services/server/alert.html`, loaded with `?raw`, typed by `src/main/env.d.ts`) at every id, and the page reads the id from its path. Nothing is stored for it, so deleting, cloning or importing alerts needs no cleanup, and cloned profiles share their alerts' overlays. The media folder is mounted at `/alerts/media/` (`express.static`, so videos get byte ranges).
- The window's queue times the media, so it shows with the alert's sound and waits its turn. `ALERT_AUDIO` carries `media`, and the item's place lasts `max(sound length / speed, duration)` (`minDuration`). A media-only alert sends no `data` and holds a silent place (`AudioQueue.wait`). Once the item has its place, `queueAlert` (`renderer/src/helper/alerts.ts`) emits `SHOW_ALERT_MEDIA` with the delay, and `AlertsService.showMedia` sends `tokio:alert:media` to the alert's overlay (`sendToClient`) when it's over. Main keeps the timers, so `clear` can drop the media still waiting.
- The page shows one at a time, and removes it after `duration`, when a video ends, or on `tokio:alert:clear`. It ignores the live events every client gets. A video plays at its own volume (`media.volume`, which `queueAlert` sends with `SHOW_ALERT_MEDIA`), muted if the browser refuses sound.
- `AlertMediaSchema.volume` (0–100, default 100) is the video's, apart from the alert's `volume`, the sound's. Media saved before it had one gets the alert's volume, as it played then (`withSavedMedia`, a preprocess on `AlertSchema`). Another file keeps it, as it keeps the duration.
- `withSavedMedia` also cuts a video saved before cuts (no `start`) at its old `duration`, so it stops where it did, unless its `match_video` (Match video, which the cut replaced) was on. The schema no longer has `match_video`.
- Media rides on the alert's audio: a missing sound or a blocked or failed message skips it too. A file missing on disk (a profile imported from another computer) shows nothing, with no notice.
- The Alerts page applies a `SET_ALERT_MEDIA` reply only while that alert waits for one (`askedMedia`, apart from the sounds' `asked`). The `MediaPicker` only picks: it closes after a pick, and once a browsed file for its alert arrives. `IMPORT_MEDIA` also replies `GET_MEDIA`. Another file keeps the alert's duration.
- The `MediaPicker` shows the library as thumbnails, the alert's file marked with `aria-current` (the sound picker's library marks its sound the same way). The window loads them from `tokio-media:///<file>` (`getMediaPreviewURL`): main registers `MEDIA_SCHEME` with `stream` for videos and serves only images and videos right in the media folder (`mediaFile`, `src/main/helper/preview.ts`). The CSP allows it in `img-src` and `media-src`. A video shows its first frame (`#t=0.1`).
- Every video player loads its own URL, `useVideoURL(file)` (`components/MediaPicker`, a `?player=` query from `useId`, which `mediaFile` ignores): Chromium fails every player that loads one `tokio-media:` URL at once (error 4), and the URL stays failed. An alert's row thumbnail and its editor's preview, or a Media Library row and its **Full size**, load together. Videos come in the parts their players ask for (`videoResponse`, `src/main/helper/preview.ts`, 206 with `Content-Range`): Chromium seeks only in media served in parts, and net.fetch of a `file:` URL answers a Range with the whole file, so the editor's cut couldn't seek, nor `#t=0.1` show its frame. Images and sounds still go through net.fetch.
- The `MediaPicker`'s search is a `SearchToggle` in its title row (`Modal`'s `actions`), like the overlay gallery's. Its footer is Clear, Cancel and **Browse file...**.
- The editor's **Media** tab is the file's preview (`MediaPreview`, 288px, or a dashed box of its size without media: an image, or a video with a play/pause button on its bottom left that plays it right there at its volume, which follows the slider while it plays, and its cut's length on its bottom right) with a column beside it: the name and **Change...**, the **Duration** for an image or a video of unknown length, and the video's volume ("Video volume"). The overlay URL (`CopyField`), the alert's rather than the file's, runs under both. A video whose length the preview read gets a **Cut** above it: `CutTrack` without `peaks` (a plain, shorter track), whose `onSeek` pauses the preview and shows the frame a handle moved to (Reset cut shows 0). The preview opens on the cut's start, and its play button plays the cut from its start and pauses at its end (a timer from `playing`), the line sweeping it. Such a video has no Duration to type: an effect sets it to the length of the part that plays (`cutPart`, `pages/Alerts/helper.ts`, the length the preview read on `loadedmetadata`), to the hundredth, from 1 to `MEDIA_MAX_DURATION`, also for a video picked or cut later. A video whose length is unknown (some recordings don't say it, or the preview fails, `onError`) can't be cut and keeps a typed Duration; none shows while the preview reads it. The tab sets it, so a file browsed while it isn't shown keeps the old duration until it is. The row shows the duration with `formatAdded`. `MEDIA_DURATION` and `MEDIA_MAX_DURATION` are next to the schema, in `src/common/schemas/profile.ts`.
- The picker's tiles and the Media Library's rows show a video's length with `SoundLength`, from the length its thumbnail read (`onLength`), as the sound lists do. A space between the tile's name and its length keeps the button's name "Clip 12s".
- **Settings → Media Library** (`MediaSection`) lists the library like the Sound Library: each row a thumbnail (`MediaThumbnail`, from the picker), the name, **Full size** (a `Modal` with the image or the video playing) and **Delete**. **Upload** sends `UPLOAD_MEDIA`, which shares the `upload` loop with `UPLOAD_SOUNDS`.
- `useAlertTest` queues a test of media alone without asking main. A test with a sound or message passes the request's `media` to `queueAlert` on the reply. Stopping a test leaves its media on the overlay until it ends.
