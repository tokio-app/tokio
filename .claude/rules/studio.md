# Overlay Studio

**Tools → Overlay Studio** (`pages/Studio`, `/tools/studio`) lists the streamer's own overlays, each with its number of elements, built from elements alone or in groups; each opens in the editor (`pages/StudioEditor`, `/tools/studio/$id`). They live in `settings.studio`, shared by every profile like the overlay library, and each is served at `/studio/<id>/` (`STUDIO_PATH`, `getStudioOverlayURL`, `NOTE: DO NOT CHANGE`), one browser source.

## Architecture

- One renderer for the stream and the editor: `src/common/studio/stage.ts` (`createStage(root, { slots, editing })` → `update`, `react`, `slot`, `fit`, `destroy`), in the markup `src/common/studio/overlay.css`'s header describes. `ServerService` inlines both into `studio.html`; the editor mounts the same stage (`StudioStage.tsx`) and imports the stylesheet globally.
- It keeps one node per stage layer (`.studio-layer`, the whole canvas, stacked by `z-index`): a group's, or an element's on its own, whose layer id is the element's own id. Inside it, one node per element id, updated in place. It never moves a node or resets an iframe's `src` unless the element's kind or source changed, or it went into, out of or between groups: Chromium reloads a moved iframe, and views come on every autosave and every numbers push.
- A layer's node lets the pointer through (`pointer-events: none`); its elements catch it. A group's takes the group's `opacity`, over its elements' own.
- An element's node takes its `rotation` as an inline `transform`, around its center, so its entrance and animation (on `.studio-body`) turn with it. Its flips come after it in the same `transform` (`scale(-1, 1)`), so it mirrors along its own sides, then turns.
- `studioView(overlay, deps)` (`src/common/util/studio.ts`) resolves every element into one stage layer per group or standalone element, and leaves hidden groups and elements out; a `z` is the place among all of them, hidden ones included, so hiding one doesn't renumber the rest. Main (`src/main/helper/studio.ts`) and the editor call it with their own `deps`, so the page never fills text or matches triggers.
- The page gets the stage through `stage.ts?script` (`inlineScript` in `electron.vite.config.ts`, also in Vitest's main project, typed in `src/main/env.d.ts`): esbuild compiles the file alone, not bundled, to an IIFE on the global `TokioStage`, `target: "chrome103"`. So `stage.ts` imports only types, and uses nothing newer than Chromium 103 (OBS 30): no `toSorted`, `Object.groupBy` and the like. `overlay.css` has the same limit: no `:has()`, nesting, subgrid, container queries or `color-mix()`.
- `overlay.css` loads globally in the windows, so every class starts with `studio-`. Backgrounds stay transparent and nothing sets `color-scheme`: Chromium paints an iframe opaque when its `color-scheme` differs from its page's.
- In the editor (`slots: true`) each embed is an empty, stable `.studio-slot` that React portals its preview into; `editing: true` keeps the elements that wait for an event on screen (`.studio-waits`) and plays a react in place, with no timer to hide it.
- A cropped or rounded picture is laid out by the stage itself (`layoutPicture`), since OBS 30's Chromium 103 has no `object-view-box`: from its own size (`naturalWidth`, a video's `videoWidth`), the part it keeps fits its box as contain fits a whole one, all of it placed around that part (`.studio-cut`) and clipped with `clip-path: inset(… round …)`. It's hidden until its size is read, so it never shows uncut, and laid out again on `load` or `loadedmetadata` (a gift a viewer sends, a new file). A viewer's crop covers its circle instead (`layoutAvatar`), and its `border-radius` follows its rounding. A plain picture keeps the stylesheet's contain. `Stage.picture(id)` gives the picture's own size, for the editor's crops.
- A video plays the part its element cuts (`start`, `end`; `partOf` copies `cutPart`'s rule, since the file imports only types), timed by its own clock as `alert.html` times an alert's: a timeout wakes at the part's end and arms again while the clock lags, and the video's own end is `ended`'s.
  - With `loop` it plays again from the part's start. An uncut one keeps the browser's own seamless `loop`, which goes back to 0.
  - Without, it stops there (`done`). On stream, an element that showed on an event leaves then: its Seconds hide it only if it isn't playing, as the alert page's duration does. One that moves on an event plays its part again on each.
  - Every view runs `playback`, which leaves a `done` video where it stopped and never plays an `ended` one (`play()` would start it at 0). An `ended` that comes after a react sought back is dropped.
  - A changed source, cut or loop (the entry's `cut`) starts it over, and so does a new player (an image, then Undo).
- **Sound:** a video plays at its `volume`.
  - A browser that refuses sound (a tab before a click; OBS doesn't) rejects `play()` with `NotAllowedError`. The stage then plays it muted, and later ones start muted (`quiet`: the policy is the page's), until a `click` unmutes them. A press isn't a user activation for touch, and Chromium pauses a video unmuted without one.
  - The editor's stage starts `silent`; `Stage.sound(on)` (Canvas sound) unmutes its videos. The app's `autoplay-policy` switch (`src/main/index.ts`) lets them play sound with no click.
- `Stage.hold(id, seconds)` is the editor's too: it pauses an element's video on that frame (`still`) and keeps it there through `fill` and `playback`, until a timer (`letGo`) plays its part again from its start. `Stage.replay(id)` plays it again from its start at once, for **Play again**.

## Data

- An overlay's `items` go back to front: groups (`StudioGroupSchema`, `kind: "group"`) and elements on their own. A group's `elements` go back to front too, and groups don't hold groups. A group has a `name`, `locked` (the editor's canvas can't pick, move or resize its elements), `hidden` (off the canvas and the stream), `folded` (the editor's list), `rotation` (its frame's turn in the editor), `flip_x` and `flip_y` (whether its elements were mirrored as one, for the toggles) and `opacity` (its layer's, on stream too); its elements keep the result of moving, scaling, turning and mirroring it; an element (`StudioElementSchema`) is one of the seven kinds, with its `label` (its name in the editor, `""` for what it shows; not `name`, which is a media element's file name), box, `rotation` (degrees clockwise around its center, -180 to 180), `flip_x` and `flip_y` (mirrored left to right, top to bottom), opacity, its own `locked` and `hidden`, and When. An element is locked or hidden when it or its group is. Ids are unique across groups and elements both.
- `StudioOverlaySchema` (`src/common/schemas/studio.ts`) is the strict schema, for IPC and the store's mutators. It validates without transforming (no trims, clamps, rounding or `.catch`): `useAutosave` settles a save only when main's echo deep-equals what it sent. For the same reason the editor builds only schema-shaped objects, since Zod drops unknown keys.
- `StoredStudioOverlaysSchema`, for `settings.studio` (`.default([])`), drops an element, then a group, then an overlay, that doesn't parse (a newer Tokio's kind after a downgrade, a hand edit) instead of failing the store, which would reset every setting. An overlay saved before groups, with `layers`, loads with each layer as a group (`withItems`).
- A picture element (media, gift, emote and viewer, `StudioPictureKind`) also has `crop` (`{ left, top, right, bottom }`, the fractions of the picture cut from each side, which can't meet) and `rounding` (its corners, a % of the shorter side of what shows: 50 is round), a viewer's 50 by default, today's circle. Its view's `crop` is null when it keeps all of the picture.
- A media element also has its video's `start` and `end` (the alerts' `CUT`: seconds, a null end playing to the end), `loop` (on by default) and `volume` (0 to 100, 100 by default); an image ignores them. Another file (`setMedia`) starts uncut and keeps its volume and loop, as alerts do.
- An overlay also saves the editor's `rulers` (shown or not, off for a new one) and `guides` (`{ x, y, locked }`: where each vertical guide crosses the top edge and each horizontal one the left edge, in canvas px, in the order they were added). The stream never gets either. The stored schema drops guides it can't read (`.catch`), not the overlay.
- Caps: `STUDIO_MAX_GROUPS` (100), `STUDIO_MAX_ELEMENTS` (100, grouped or not), `STUDIO_TEXT_MAX_LENGTH` (500), `STUDIO_NAME_MAX_LENGTH` (60, for groups' and elements' names too), `STUDIO_MAX_SECONDS` (60), `STUDIO_MAX_FONT_SIZE` (400), `STUDIO_MAX_GUIDES` (50 across, 50 down). Each autosave rewrites and flushes the whole `tokio.json`, so keep them tight.
- A media element saves a library basename; the only URL an element saves is an emote's `image`, kept to `https:`. Text goes in with `textContent`, fonts as one `fontFamily` (`JSON.stringify`), colors as CSS variables (hex only).
- The list page picks the id (`crypto.randomUUID()`) and the name ("Overlay N", `newOverlayName`). `addStudioOverlay` refuses an id that isn't a UUID or is taken, since it becomes the URL and the socket id; `saveStudioOverlay` refuses one it doesn't hold. The mutators return whether the store file was written; a false still keeps the change in memory, then `saveFailure()`.
- Handlers in `src/main/events/pages/studio.ts` (`createStack("overlays")`): `GET_STUDIO`, `ADD_STUDIO_OVERLAY`, `SAVE_STUDIO_OVERLAY` (autosave, no notice), `REMOVE_STUDIO_OVERLAY`, `TEST_STUDIO_OVERLAY` (`notify: false`). Every one replies `STUDIO` with the list main holds, failures included.

## Reactions

- Each element reacts on its own: its `when` is `always`, `event` (hidden until its trigger, then `seconds` with its `entrance`) or `animate` (always on screen, plays its `animation` on each trigger). A group doesn't react; it only groups.
- `StudioService.handle` runs `streakOf` once per event, then `matchesTrigger` per element that reacts, both from `@/helper/triggers` like alerts. On Like an element reacts each time the likes from every viewer pass another `likes_every` (default 100, the rest carried over, at most once per event), counted per element.
- A gift or emote element with none picked (`gift: null`, `emote: null`, a new one's default, on a Gift or Emote trigger) shows what its event sent: `sentPicture` reads the gift's `image`, or the message's emote its trigger names (else the first), for the react's `picture` and, from what it kept, for its view (`sent` in `StudioViewDeps`). Emote samples (`generateEmoteChatSample`) always hold an emote.
- It keeps each element's last event values and viewer (picture, name) until Tokio quits, so a reloaded page shows them. `kept(element)` returns them only while the element's trigger type is the one they came from. Before the first event, the trigger's variables read `""`.
- Hidden elements, and those of a hidden group, still react and keep their values: their page ignores reacts for elements it doesn't draw, and they have the latest event when they show again.
- `forget(id)` on remove; `clear()` on `DISCONNECTED` drops the gift streaks and like counts, not the kept values.
- Another react while an element shows restarts its entrance and its `seconds` with the newest values; an `animate` element plays its animation again from the start.
- Test (the editor's play button) plays every element that reacts once, with samples: the editor on its own stage (fresh `Samples`), and `TEST_STUDIO_OVERLAY` on stream (`StudioService.test`, `generateTikTokEventSample`). Test's samples are never kept, so a reload shows the real last event.

## Live numbers

- `{likes}`, `{viewers}`, `{followers}`, `{shares}`, `{coins}` in any text, compact in the app's language (`formatStat`, now in `src/common/util/format.ts`). `fillStudioText` fills them first (names checked with `Object.hasOwn`), then the trigger's values in one `formatAlertMessage` pass, so a viewer's comment that says `{likes}` stays as typed.
- `registerStudio` (`src/main/events/init.ts`) is registered after `registerConnectorEvents`: the connector runs listeners in order, synchronously, so the stats and the LIVE session already hold the event when the studio reads them.
- One listener per trigger serves both the reacts and the numbers (GIFT and CHAT have many listeners already; Node warns past 10). Emote triggers are matched on chat; `MUSIC_CHANGE` comes from `music.onSongChange`, once per song as alerts take it.
- On `LIKE`, `ROOM_USER`, the `ACTIVITY_EVENTS` and `CONNECTED`, a throttle (`STATS_THROTTLE_MS`) re-sends the views of the overlays whose texts use a number (`showsNumbers`), built when it fires. `DISCONNECTED` cancels it, so a late push can't show the LIVE's numbers over the zeros, and sends them at once.
- `liveNumbers`: `likes` and `viewers` from `getLiveStats()`, the rest from `live.getSession().totals`, 0 while `startedAt === null`. The editor shows `SAMPLE_NUMBERS`, never the LIVE's.
- Views are also re-sent on `SAVE_STUDIO_OVERLAY` (that one, even when the write failed), on `onLanguage`, after a gift refresh and after `recordGift` saves a gift TikTok's list misses, after `REMOVE_MEDIA`, and after `REMOVE_OVERLAY` and `INSTALL_OVERLAY`, since an embed's resolution changes.

## Messages and connections

- The page connects with the socket id `studio:<id>` (`studioSocketId`, `STUDIO_SOCKET_PREFIX`); `onConnect` sends it its view. It reconnects after 2 s and ignores the LIVE events every overlay gets.
- Two messages, private to the page like `tokio:timer` (not in `TokioPayload`): `tokio:studio` (`{ view: StudioView | null }`) and `tokio:studio:react` (`{ element, text?, picture?, name? }`).
- A removed overlay, or an id main doesn't hold, gets `{ view: null }`, which clears the page.

## Embeds

- On stream each is an iframe at its real path: `/<id>/` for an installed overlay, `/alerts/<id>/`, `/timer/`, `/games/minecraft/`, or one column's `/games/minecraft/<column>/`. An installed overlay that isn't served gets `src: null` and draws nothing; reinstalling it (official overlays keep their id) re-sends the views and it shows again.
- A games embed's `column` (1 to `GAMES_OVERLAY_MAX_COLUMNS`) places one column on its own, `null` all of them. The picker offers each column once the games overlay has more than one, "row" when its layout is horizontal as the Games page says it, plus one an element kept after the columns dropped. The editor's preview then draws only that column's rows, as its only column.
- `StudioSource` has one union member per `type`, so a chain of `if`s on `type` narrows to the last.
- In the editor (`Embed.tsx`): an installed overlay previews from disk in a named `Iframe` with `transparent` (`?transparent`, see `.claude/rules/overlays.md`), with its own sample data. The countdown timer and the games overlay show their pages' previews (`TimerPreview` and `OverlayPreview` with `bare`) in a `FitBox` scaled to the element's box. An alert's overlay is a labeled box, since it shows media only while its alert plays.
- Previews can't frame the real pages live: the app's session cancels every WebSocket to the overlay server (`initSession` in `src/main/handler/app.ts`, `URL_FILTER`).
- An overlay embedded twice, in one studio overlay or two, shares its settings and saved state, as two browser sources of it do.
- An embed moved into, out of or between groups reloads, as its node moves.

## Media sweep

- `REMOVE_MEDIA` (`pages/alerts.ts`) clears the file from every studio media element, grouped or not (`removeFromStudio`: the element stays, empty), before deleting it, re-sends the views, and keeps the file with `saveFailure()` when the store can't be written.
- On `ALERT_FILE_REMOVED` for media the editor clears that file from its elements too, so its next save can't bring it back, and from every step of its history (`sweep`), so an undo can't either.

## The editor

- The panel is **Elements** (the user's call, renamed from Layers so it says what it holds): groups, and elements on their own. Its list is a tree with the front on top: the overlay's items, each group a tray (a hairline around its row and its elements, rose while the group is selected, as the canvas outlines its frame) with its elements, unless it's folded, set in 20 px under its row; the overlay keeps both back to front.
- An element's row shows a thumbnail of what it draws (`LayerThumb`, from the element itself, so hidden ones keep theirs: a picture, a shape in miniature in its colors, a text's `textSample` in its font and color; an icon for what only the stream knows, the gift or emote a viewer sends, a viewer's picture, an embed), its name, and a second line only while it reacts: the event, after a dot in the LIVE tally's rose. Its kind and When are said to screen readers only (`sr_only`). A folded or empty group shows its count under its name. The grip, lock and eye show on hover, focus and the selection, and a lock or an eye that's on always shows; a hidden row dims.
- A row's grip drags it. An element drops among a group's elements, onto a group's row (into it, in front; the row's top quarter puts it in front of the group, on its own, so an element can leave a group between two groups), or onto a standalone element's row (on its own, in its place). A group drops only among the items: over a group's element, it takes that group's place. Off the rows, in an 8 px gap between items or the room under the last (the list fills the panel), a group or an element drops on its own in front of the item below, or at the back past the last: that's how an element leaves a group (the user asked). A drop among the items draws its line in the gap outside a tray or an element on its own (`drop_before`, `drop_after`), one into a group inside its tray (`layer_before`, `layer_after`). ArrowUp and ArrowDown move a row a step (toward the front is up), taking the focus back after a `flushSync` as the games builder does: a group steps past its neighbor, an element steps within its group and out of it past its edge, and an element on its own steps into a group it meets, which unfolds.
- A group's row folds it, locks it and hides it; an element's row locks and hides it, and shows its `label`, or what it shows when that's blank. Lock is the canvas's only: the list and the settings still change a locked group or element (the user's call, as OBS locks).
- The header's **+** menu adds a group ("Group N", in front) or an element, into the selected group or the selected element's group, else on its own in front. At the caps its items are disabled, with a note saying why. Its trash deletes the selection at once, a group with its elements too, since Undo brings it back (the user's call; a dialog asked while nothing could). The menu sits in the header because the panel scrolls (`overflow-y: auto`), which would clip one per row.
- Delete, and Backspace (a Mac's delete key), delete the selection as the trash does, on the canvas (`StudioStage`'s `onKeyDown`, but not a locked one, as the arrows leave it) and in the list (its `ul`'s `onKeyDown`, which also takes a locked one, as the trash does). From the list, the focus goes on to the row after it, or before it at the end (`data-select`, after a `flushSync`), so the keyboard can go on. Fields never see it, so it still edits their text.
- Ctrl+C copies the selected group or element and Ctrl+X cuts it, Cmd too; Ctrl+V pastes a copy with new ids (`copyItem`) where the **+** menu adds: an element into the selected group or the selected element's group, which unfolds, else on its own in front, and a group on its own in front. The copy is selected. Each paste lands 20 px further right and down (`PASTE_STEP`), the first after a cut where it was. The clipboard is the editor module's own, not the system's, kept until Tokio quits, so a copy pastes into another overlay too. Fields keep their own copy, cut and paste (`TYPED_FIELDS`, number fields too), and so does text selected on the page. Nothing pastes past the caps.
- `selected` is a group's id or an element's: ids are unique across both. A click in the Elements list past the items (under them or in a gap, not on a tray) clears it (the user's call), as Escape and a click on the empty canvas do. The settings pane follows it: `GroupSettings` (the name, and how it moves, or that it's locked or hidden), `ElementSettings` (its **Name**, saved as `label`, with the row's name as placeholder, then the kind's fields, box, flips, opacity and When) or, with none, `OverlaySettings`.
- The stage picks by geometry, not the DOM: the pointer in canvas px (`getBoundingClientRect` and `fit()`), then the front-most element whose box, turned, holds it, skipping locked groups and elements (`locked` holds both ids), so a click goes through them. Picking an element in a folded group unfolds it.
- A standalone element's stage layer has the element's id, so the stage takes a selected id as an element first: only a group is a selected layer, with the group's outline and drag.
- An element selected: drag moves it, a handle on each corner resizes it with the opposite corner kept where it is on the canvas (`resizeBox`, along a turned element's own sides), the round handle above its top turns it around its center (`turnTo`, Shift in steps of 15), arrows nudge 1 px (Shift 10), Escape deselects, Delete deletes it. None of it on a locked element or group, which keeps the outline, turned with it, but no handles. Boxes are whole px, width and height at least 1 (`roundBox`); turns are whole degrees, never -0, which would come back from main as 0.
- A text's box can't get shorter than its words on the canvas, nor narrower than its longest word, so its height can't run away (both the user's calls). `textHeight` measures the words with a `Range` on the stage, at the width the resize gives and unturned, so wrapped lines count; `textWidth` measures the text at `min-content` with `overflow-wrap: normal`, since on stream a word too long for its box breaks anywhere. A change to a text's words in its settings (`WORDS`: text, font, size, bold) grows its box to them too, once the stage has drawn them (the editor's `fitting` ref, then `fitText` in a layout effect after the stage's update), around where they show so they stay put: across from the side they line up with, and from the middle, where they sit. It never shrinks the box, and Test's samples don't grow it.
- The box fields keep a text to its words too (the user's call): the stage measures the selected text after each change (`onWords`: its longest word, its words' height at its width, and `heightAt` for another width), Width and Height take them as their `min` (a typed number under it isn't reported, a drag stops there), and a narrower Width grows the Height to the words. `BoxFields`' `sizing` keeps the size a run of changes to one field began from, so the Height a narrower Width gave comes back with a wider one, and keystrokes on the way to a number (the 45 of 450) don't leave it tall.
- Under the box's fields, **Flip** (two toggles beside Rotation) mirrors the element, and **Align to the canvas** moves it to an edge or a middle of the canvas (`alignTo`), a turned element by how it shows, its `extent`.
- **Cropping:** a picture's settings (`PictureFields`) have **Crop (%)**, the % off each side, with **Crop on the canvas** and **Reset the crop** on its title's row, and **Round corners**, a slider to 50%. Cropping never moves or scales a media, gift or emote picture: its box becomes the part it keeps (`recrop`, from `Stage.picture`), empty bands gone; Reset grows it back. A viewer's picture fills its circle, so its crop picks the part of each viewer's picture that shows there, its box kept, and it has no canvas crop. Another file, gift or emote starts uncropped (`pictureSource`); its corners stay.
- **On the canvas** (media, gifts and emotes, not locked): a double-click or Crop starts it (the editor's `cropping`). The stage draws the element whole and unrounded where all of its picture is (`pictureFrames`, `boxOn`), and over it the crop frame, turned with it and rounded as it will be, with 8 handles (`data-crop`) and a `box-shadow` that dims the rest. A handle drags its side or corner within the picture, at least 8 canvas px apart, and a press inside the frame drags the picture under it (`cropDrag`). Enter, a click past the frame or another selection keeps it, as one step (`cropOf`); Escape cancels. Undo, Redo, copy and paste wait meanwhile. The list's thumbnails show the crop through `object-view-box`, which the app's own Chromium has.
- **A video's settings** (`VideoFields`, keyed by its file), under the file's button. The canvas is its preview: the user asked why a second one, so the alerts' `MediaPreview` was dropped.
  - **Cut** is the alerts' `CutTrack` in a titled block, with **Reset the cut** on its row, shown once a hidden player has read the length, as `SoundLength` reads a sound's. A video that doesn't say its length can't be cut.
  - While a handle moves (a drag, the arrows, a typed second), the canvas holds the video still on that frame (`Stage.hold`, `onSeek`), and 600 ms after the last move (`HOLD_MS`) it plays its cut again from its start. The stage lets go on its own, so a selection that changes mid-drag can't leave it frozen.
  - **Loop**. While it's off, **Play again** sits at the row's end (the user asked: without Loop the canvas's video stops at its end) and plays the cut once more from its start (`Stage.replay`, `onPlay`).
  - **Volume (N%)**.
  - The length stays in the component's state: no effect writes the overlay, since that would make an undo step and drop Redo.
  - On an event without Loop, **Seconds on screen** says **Until the video ends** in its number's place.
- **Canvas sound**, the speaker before the rulers' toggle under the canvas, plays the canvas's videos with their sound (`Stage.sound`), Test's included. It's off each time the editor opens (the user's call), it isn't saved or an undo step, and it's disabled while the canvas shows no video.
- **Link width and height**, the toggle between Width and Height, makes them change together in proportion, in the fields and on the canvas's corners (`resizeBox`'s `even`, along the box's diagonal; a text still can't get smaller than its words). It's the editor's `linked`, for every element and group until the editor closes, not saved. The fields take the proportions from the size they started at (`linking` in `BoxFields`), so a dragged field doesn't drift by rounding; a size they didn't make (a drag, another element; within a px, as a group's frame comes back from its elements' whole px) starts over.
- The editor's number fields drag (`NumberInput`'s `scrub`; other pages' don't): held a quarter second or dragged, the cursor turns to a double arrow (not on a click, which the user saw flash), and moving right or left raises or lowers the value a step per screen px, 10 with Shift, past 3 px. The press doesn't focus the field: a click without that much movement does, with its number selected, and a field already being typed in selects text as usual.
- A click focuses the stage without the focus ring (`focus({ focusVisible: false })`): Chromium rang the whole canvas after a field had the focus, which the user found meaningless. Tab still rings it, for the arrows.
- The stage cuts what passes its edges (`overflow: clip`), so a selection past the canvas can't stretch the page. Inside it, `.stage_scroll` scrolls a zoomed canvas, and `.stage_sizer` (at least the view's size, the canvas's with its margin when zoomed past it) keeps the canvas and the selection 40 px inside its edges, so the handles of an element at the canvas's edge stay in view, the rotate handle 32 px past its side. The sizer cuts what passes it too, so an element far off the canvas can't stretch the scroll.
- The rotate handle's cursor is a curved arrow with a head at each end, black on a white halo like the system's (`rotateCursor`: an SVG `data:` URL at 1x and 2x in `image-set`, so the CSP's `img-src` keeps `data:`), turned by the element's or the group's rotation in steps of 5° so it lies along the circle around the middle. While it turns, the stage keeps it (`held`) and it follows the turn. The user found the old circular arrow weird.
- Over what a press would drag (an unlocked element, or the selected group's outline: `pickElement` and `holdsTurned` on each pointer move), the stage shows the move cursor, and a drag keeps its cursor on the whole stage while the pointer leaves what it holds (`held`: move, a corner's resize, or rotate). The corner handles' resize cursors don't turn with the element.
- A group selected (from the list) outlines its frame (`groupFrame`: the smallest box, turned by its `rotation`, around its shown elements) with an element's handles. Dragging inside it, after 3 screen px, moves its elements by one rounded delta, and so do the arrows; a click without that much movement picks what's under it. A locked one's outline picks at once, past it, with no handles, and an empty or hidden one has no outline, so the arrows move nothing.
- Its corners and rotate handle, and its settings' box fields (`BoxFields`, shared with elements), move, scale and turn its elements into the new frame (`reframe`: each keeps its place in the frame, its sides scaled as the frame's are along them). Every step of a drag, or of a run of changes in its fields (`run` in `GroupSettings`, while the frame is still the one the last change asked for, within a px), goes from the elements the first step found (`reframing`, by the identity of the frame it began from), so rounding doesn't add up: without it, small elements stalled at their rounded size. That keeps the state update pure enough for React to run twice, as it does in development under `StrictMode`: an earlier version keyed it by the elements it last made, and each run added a turn to the last, so a group spun past the pointer. Its flips mirror them across it (`flipInFrame`), Align moves them, and Opacity fades its layer. Scaling a group can squash a text below its words.
- An element that waits for an event (`when: "event"`) gets a dashed hairline (`.studio-waits`), so it can be placed. Hairlines inside the scaled canvas divide by `--canvas-scale` to stay one screen px.
- The editor's body mounts only once the `STUDIO` list holds its id; the list page opens a new overlay only then, so a failed add opens nothing. A deleted id shows an `EmptyState` with the way back.
- `.stage .stage_root` takes two classes on purpose: the stream stylesheet's `.studio-stage` on the same element sets `position: relative`, which left the root as tall as its absolute canvas (0) and the canvas scaled to 0, whatever order the two stylesheets load in.
- The editor's page uses `<Page width="full">`.

## Undo and redo

- `useHistory` (`StudioEditor/useHistory.ts`) keeps the overlay as it was before each step, up to 100, while the editor is open. Every edit goes through its `change` (the editor's `setOverlay`); the autosave saves an undo or a redo like any change, so the stream follows.
- A step is a drag on the canvas, press to release, pauses included (`hold`, from `StudioStage`'s `onHold`), or else a run of changes less than 500 ms apart from one control (`document.activeElement`) with one selection: a number typed, a field dragged, a text growing to its words. Another control, another selection or a pause starts the next one, and so does a change after an undo, which drops what Redo would bring back.
- It records in the event, not in a state updater, so StrictMode's double updaters can't push a step twice. An undo skips steps that changed nothing.
- Folding a group and showing the rulers aren't steps: the list's arrow, an unfold from a pick, the rulers' toggle and Ctrl+R go around the history (`fold` and `onRulers`, through the autosave's own setter), and an undo or redo leaves every group folded and the rulers as they are now (`withView`). The selection, the zoom, Link and Test aren't steps either. The guides are: each add, move, removal, lock or clear, on the canvas or in the list, is one.
- The header's **Undo** and **Redo** (titled with their keys), and Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z (Cmd too) on the window. Text fields keep their own Ctrl+Z (`TEXT_FIELDS`); number fields, checkboxes, radios and sliders take the editor's; a dialog takes none. The listener prevents what it takes: Electron's default Edit menu, which the app keeps, gets only the keys the page didn't handle.

## Zoom, rulers and guides

- `zoom` in `StudioStage` is the canvas's scale, `null` while it fits the stage. Zoomed, `.stage_area` gets the canvas's size at that scale, centered in the sizer while the view has room, and the stage's own `fit()` reads the scale from the root's size, so picking, dragging, the outline, text measuring and hairlines work unchanged. `stage.ts`, the stream's renderer, knows nothing of it.
- The bar under the canvas: its size, then the rulers' toggle, zoom out, the level (an `<output>` named Zoom, the real scale: 100% is a canvas px per screen px), zoom in and **Zoom to fit**. The buttons and Ctrl+=, Ctrl++ and Ctrl+- (Cmd too) step through `ZOOMS` (10 to 400%) around the stage's middle; Ctrl+0 fits again. Inside the editor those keys zoom the canvas instead of the window: the page prevents them before Electron's View menu would see them. Not in a dialog.
- Ctrl or Cmd with the wheel, and a trackpad's pinch, zoom smoothly around the pointer (a native `wheel` listener with `passive: false`, since React's can't prevent the scroll). Every zoom keeps the canvas point under the pointer, or the middle, where it was: `anchor`, scrolled back in a layout effect that also calls `setFit`, so the outline doesn't lag a frame. It zooms in a `flushSync`, so the wheel's next step starts from this one.
- Zoomed past the stage, the view scrolls (the wheel, Shift and the wheel, its scrollbars). A press on the scrollbars (its target is `.stage_scroll` itself) picks nothing. Space held with the canvas focused, or nothing, makes a drag pan it (`held` is `"pan"`; a hand cursor, closed while dragging, over the handles too), selecting and moving nothing; a field or a button keeps its own Space.
- The rulers (`Rulers.tsx`) are each overlay's own (`rulers`, off for a new one). Their toggle, **Rulers and guides**, and Ctrl+R (Cmd too, not in a dialog) show and hide them with the guides. Inside the editor Ctrl+R doesn't reload the window: the page prevents it before Electron's View menu sees it, and Ctrl+Shift+R still force-reloads.
- They run along the stage's top and left in canvas px from the canvas's top-left corner, a number every 20 to 1000 px so they're at least 60 screen px apart, and a tick every tenth (`rulerTicks`). Their own state follows the scroll (in a `flushSync`, so they move in the canvas's frame) and the fit, so a scroll draws only them: `StudioStage`'s layout effects measure text on every render.
- **Guides** show only with the rulers. Ctrl or Cmd and a press on the top ruler drags out a horizontal guide, on the left one a vertical guide: `start` captures the pointer on the stage element, so the stage's handlers take a ruler's drag. Let go over the view (the scroller's client area), it stays at whole px; off it, nothing. Each ruler's tooltip (an SVG `<title>`) says how.
- Ctrl or Cmd and a press within 4 screen px of a guide (`GUIDE_REACH`, `nearGuide`), before the handles and the elements, moves it, with `col-resize` or `row-resize` over it while Ctrl is down. Dropped off the view, it goes. A drop saves once (`withGuide`), when it changed. While dragged, a guide sticks to the canvas's edges and middle and every element's (`guideAt`, not with Alt), and the ruler that measures it shows its place in an Info label (`mark`).
- They're drawn in `.stage_area`, so they follow the zoom and the scroll natively: a screen px of `--info` across the whole sizer (`-100vh` or `-100vw` past the area, cut by the sizer's `overflow: clip`), under the rose lines and the selection.
- Locked (`guides.locked`), the canvas can't add, move or remove them; the list still changes them, as an element's lock works. Past `STUDIO_MAX_GUIDES` on an axis, a ruler's drag adds nothing and the list's add button is disabled.
- With nothing selected and the rulers on, `OverlaySettings` lists them under **Guides**: vertical then horizontal, in the order added, each one's place in a number field (`integer`, `scrub`) with a remove button, then **Add vertical** and **Add horizontal** (at the canvas's middle), and on the title's row **Lock guides** and **Remove all guides**.

## Sticking

- A drag on the canvas sticks, always, with no setting. A moved element or group sticks when its extent's edges or middle (a turned one by how it shows, as Align goes) come within 6 screen px (`SNAP_REACH`) of a line: the nearest on each axis (`snapBox`). A group's moves by the frame it began with, before the shared delta is rounded.
- A dragged corner sticks the same way (`snapPoint` on where `resizeBox` put it, `stickCorner`). With Link on it keeps to the box's diagonal, so it sticks on the closer axis only. A text still can't get smaller than its words.
- The lines (`snapLines`, computed at the press): the canvas's edges and middle, every shown element's that the drag doesn't move (a group never sticks to its own), locked ones included, and the guides while the rulers show.
- While it lines up, a rose screen px shows each line its edges or middle meet (`metLines`, within half a px): across both boxes for an element, across the canvas, or along a guide (`met`, cleared when the drag ends).
- Alt (Option), held during a drag, turns sticking and the rose lines off for it: moves, corners and guides. On Linux desktops that move a window with Alt and a drag, Alt has to go down after the press.
- Arrows, fields, Align and turning never stick (the user's call), so a box can still sit a few px off a line.

## The user's calls

- Test plays on stream too, like the Overlays page's and the games overlay's.
- An event while an element shows restarts it with the newest values.
- Like elements react every N likes from all viewers, as game actions count them.
- Groups hold elements, and elements can also stand on their own; the panel is **Elements**. When stays on each element: a group orders, names, locks, hides and folds its elements, and moves them as one, locked ones included.
- Elements take names of their own, like groups.
- The Elements list's look: thumbnails, a tray per group with its elements set in, the event under an element that reacts, and its row's controls only when they're needed. An element leaves its group by a drop off the rows.
- Elements lock and hide like groups, and turn by a handle on the canvas or the Rotation field, like their size.
- A group resizes, turns, flips, lines up and fades like an element, and its elements take the change.
- Elements flip horizontally and vertically, and line up with the canvas's edges and middles.
- Width and height can be linked to change together, and number fields change by dragging across them while pressed.
- A text's box grows to its words when its size, font, text or bold changes, as a corner drag would, and its Width and Height fields can't make it smaller than its words.
- No ring around the whole canvas when it's clicked.
- A Like offers no viewer or count: its likes come from every viewer (`countedVariables`). Alerts keep them, as they play each viewer's batch.
- A viewer's picture offers only the triggers a viewer sends (not Envelope, Super Fan Box or Music change) and the event Whens. Gift and emote elements show what a viewer sends or one you pick (an emote from the saved ones); the first offers only the Gift or Emote trigger and the event Whens, and choosing it sets both.
- A shape has a fill and a border inside its edge, each on or off (`fill`, `color`; `border`, `border_color`, `border_width` up to `STUDIO_MAX_BORDER`). Its view's `color` is null without a fill and `border` null without one, drawn as `--studio-color` and `--studio-border`.
- A text's variables are in two rows under it: **LIVE numbers** (`STUDIO_NUMBERS`), always, and **From the event** (`studioEventVariables`), only while it reacts to one.
- Lock blocks only the canvas, clicks going through to what's under, as OBS's lock does.
- The editor's timer and games previews are close to the stream, not exact.
- Undo and Redo, from the header and with the keys, while the editor is open, with no list of the changes. Deleting a group with elements doesn't ask, since Undo brings it back.
- Delete (Supr on a Spanish keyboard) deletes the selection.
- Copy and paste, with the keys, into another overlay too.
- Zoom with its level and buttons under the canvas, Ctrl and the wheel, its keys, and Space with a drag to pan. Rulers in canvas px, off until turned on, then saved with each overlay, with Ctrl+R to show or hide them.
- Guides dragged from the rulers with Ctrl, saved with each overlay, and listed in its settings with typed places, a lock and Remove all.
- Elements stick to the guides, the canvas and each other, corners too (with Link on as well), and Alt drags freely. Arrows and fields stay exact.
- Pictures crop and round their corners up to a circle: images, videos, gifts, emotes and a viewer's picture, on the canvas and with fields (a viewer's with fields only).
- Videos cut like an alert's, with the same track and the canvas as the preview (no second player in the settings), and play their sound on stream at their own Volume. Each has a Loop switch: off, it plays once, and on an event its element leaves when it ends. The editor's canvas plays sound only with Canvas sound on, which is off each time the editor opens.
